Distribution Doesn't Fix a Content Problem: You Need Something Worth Distributing First.

Everyone wants to talk about where to post. Almost nobody asks what exactly is being posted.

By Jim Umlauf, Founder, VEWO  ·  September 17, 2026  ·  11 min read

TL;DR

Brands are constantly told to publish more on LinkedIn, Reddit, Instagram, YouTube, and email. But distribution without a content foundation creates a treadmill: another post, another video, another idea, most of it gone within days.

A better model starts with your owned property. Find a question your customer is asking, create the best answer you can, publish it on your website, then turn that answer into native formats for the channels where your customers actually spend time.

Distribution amplifies authority. It doesn't create it.

Everyone Wants to Talk About Distribution

Post more on LinkedInGet active on RedditMake ReelsStart a YouTube channelSend more emailsBe everywhere

There's no shortage of advice telling brands where to distribute content, and most of it isn't wrong. Distribution matters.

But a step usually gets skipped.

What exactly are you distributing?

If your content strategy starts with figuring out what to post on LinkedIn tomorrow, you're solving the wrong problem. Before you worry about distribution, you need something worth distributing — something useful, something authoritative, something your company actually knows, something that answers a real question your customer is asking. And ideally, something that lives somewhere you control.

Your website should be the knowledge base. Distribution is how those ideas travel.

1 The Problem

Brands Are Building on Rented Land

Social platforms are powerful. They're where people spend time, where ideas spread, and where customers discover brands they didn't know existed. But there's an important distinction between your website and most distribution channels.

✓You own

  • Your website and everything on it
  • Your structure, internal links, and data
  • Your relationship with the customer

✕You don't own

  • LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok
  • Reddit, YouTube, and their algorithms
  • The formats, the rules, or your reach

Those platforms control the algorithms, the formats, the rules, and ultimately your access to the audience. That doesn't mean you shouldn't use them — you absolutely should. It means they should play a specific role in your content system.

Takeaway

They're distribution channels. They shouldn't be your entire knowledge base.

The disposable content treadmill

Imagine your marketing strategy starts with this question every Monday: "What should we post this week?"

You brainstorm. Someone writes a LinkedIn post. Someone makes an Instagram graphic. Someone records a short video. Someone writes an email. You publish everything. Then Monday comes around again.

That's the treadmill. You're creating disposable pieces instead of building something that compounds. A LinkedIn post might perform well for a few days. An email might generate clicks. A video might reach a new audience. Those are valuable outcomes — but what did you build? Where can someone go six months later to find the information?

If every idea lives only inside individual distribution channels, your knowledge is fragmented across platforms you don't control.

2 The Asset

Your Website Should Be Your Knowledge Base

This requires changing how you think about your website. It isn't just a homepage, an About page, product pages, and a checkout. It should increasingly become the knowledge base for what your company knows. Ask yourself:

  • If your company has solved a particular problem for 10 years, where does that knowledge live?
  • If your sales team has answered the same question 500 times, where does that answer live?
  • If you've tested five approaches and know why three don't work, where is that documented?
  • If customers constantly misunderstand something about your category, have you explained it?
  • If your product works especially well for a specific use case, have you documented why?

If the answer is "our team knows that," it isn't enough. Your company's expertise has to become accessible information.

Customers can't discover knowledge that only exists inside your company. Neither can machines.

3 The Starting Point

Start With the Authoritative Answer

This connects directly to how we think about content at VEWO: find the questions, answer them, repeat. But there's an important next step.

Once you find a good question, don't immediately ask what your LinkedIn post should say. First ask: what is the best answer we can create?

Build that answer. Make it useful and specific. Include your experience and evidence. Add examples. Answer the obvious follow-ups. Explain the nuances. Then publish it somewhere you control. Now you have an authoritative source, and distribution becomes much easier.

One great answer becomes an entire distribution system

Say you sell premium outdoor furniture, and customers repeatedly ask: "What's the best outdoor furniture material for extreme heat?"

Instead of creating seven unrelated social posts this week, answer that question exceptionally well on your website: how heat affects different materials, aluminum versus teak versus steel versus synthetics, what fades, what needs maintenance, what matters in desert climates, the mistakes buyers make, and what your company has learned firsthand.

4 The Model

Source First, Distribution Second

This is the content system more brands should build.

Distribution amplifies authority. It doesn't create it.

Imagine someone posting 20 times a week about a topic they barely understand. They're distributing a lot, but that doesn't make the underlying information authoritative.

Now imagine a company with years of category experience, proprietary testing, customer data, product expertise, and a genuine point of view. That company already possesses something valuable: knowledge. The opportunity is to turn it into accessible information and then distribute it.

5 The AI Angle

Why This Matters More in the Age of AI

This isn't only a social media strategy. It's increasingly an AI visibility strategy.

AI systems need information. They need to understand who your company is, what you know, what problems you solve, what topics you're associated with, who your products are right for, how you compare with alternatives, and whether other sources reinforce those associations.

Your website is where you establish that. Distribution helps those ideas move beyond it — discussed, shared, referenced, mentioned, expanded on — creating a broader information footprint around your brand.

Takeaway

Your website and your distribution channels shouldn't compete. They should reinforce one another.

SEO, AEO, GEO, and distribution should work together

It's easy to turn these into separate initiatives. SEO over here. Social over there. Email somewhere else. PR doing its own thing. Someone experimenting with GEO. But customers don't experience your company in departments, and neither does the internet. One authoritative answer creates multiple outcomes.

6 The Craft

Distribution Isn't Copy and Paste

One important caveat. Repurposing doesn't mean pasting the first three paragraphs of your blog onto every platform.

Each channel has its own culture. What works on LinkedIn might not work on Reddit. What works in an email might not work on Instagram. A 10-minute YouTube video won't work as a 30-second Reel.

The rule
The idea travels. The format changes.

On LinkedIn, lead with an insight. On Reddit, participate in a discussion and answer a specific question. On Instagram, make it visual. On YouTube, make it a demonstration. In email, focus on one takeaway and link to the deeper explanation.

Same expertise, native execution. That's far stronger than blasting identical content everywhere.

Stop creating content from scratch for every channel

This is also an operational problem. If your social, email, video, and website teams are all independently inventing content, you're creating unnecessary work. Everyone needs ideas, research, expertise, and source material. Instead, create a common foundation.

The Content Flywheel

Done properly, this becomes a flywheel rather than a treadmill.

8 Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Should we stop posting on social until our website content is strong?

No. Keep the channels active, but change what feeds them. Start sourcing posts from authoritative answers you publish on your own site instead of inventing something new each week.

How many channels should one article support?

As many as your customers genuinely use, and no more. A strong foundational piece can usually support three to six native formats. Ten channels executed poorly is worse than three done natively.

Isn't this just repurposing content?

Repurposing usually means reformatting the same text. This is different: the website answer is the source of record, and each channel gets a native expression of the idea rather than a copy of the paragraphs.

Does distribution help AI visibility, or only search?

Both. Your site establishes what you know; off-site discussion, mentions, and references reinforce it. AI systems cross-check the wider web, so a brand talked about consistently across platforms is easier to understand and recommend.

What if we don't have anything unique to say yet?

You almost certainly do — it's just trapped in sales calls, support tickets, and product decisions. Start there: the questions your team answers repeatedly are your first authoritative articles.

9 Conclusion

Build the Source Before You Build the Megaphone

Distribution matters. LinkedIn, Reddit, YouTube, Instagram, email, and communities all matter. But none of them eliminate the need for something valuable underneath. Without strong source material, adding channels just creates more places where your team has to invent something to say. That's exhausting.

Instead: find a question worth answering. Create the best answer you can. Put the authoritative version on your website. Turn the ideas inside it into content for the channels where your customers spend time. Let those channels amplify your expertise. Learn from the response. Then answer the next question.

Because before you ask where you should distribute your content, ask a more important question: do we have something worth distributing?

Build that first. Then amplify it.

Distribution amplifies authority. It doesn't create it.
